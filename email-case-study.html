Giving the store
something to say.

The Embrava store could sell, but it could not talk back. This case adds the missing half: a welcome flow that turns sign-ups into first orders, an abandoned cart flow that turns a basket into a kit, and a launch email. Six emails, designed and coded to run in Klaviyo.

01

The brief

The store already did the hard part of retail: a colour-first catalogue, kits that are always the best deal, a free delivery nudge at £40. But once someone left the page, Embrava had no way to bring them back.

  • Turn sign-ups into first orders. The footer form collects emails. Nothing happens next.
  • Win back abandoned baskets. Someone who added a gym bag was close. One message should close the gap.
  • Launch with a proper send. The first collection deserves more than a post on social media.

One rule from the store carries over: emails should push people towards kits, because the kit logic is what raises the average order.

02

Three flows,
six emails

Two automated flows run all year. The launch email is a one-off campaign.

Welcome flow

Trigger: subscribes through the footer form. Exits: places an order.

  1. Straight awayW1 · WelcomeWho we are, plus the welcome gift: free UK delivery on a first order.
  2. Day 2W2 · Where do you train?The three kits, each 20% below the price of the pieces.
  3. Day 5W3 · Build your ownAny three pieces for 15% off, with a worked example.

Abandoned cart flow

Trigger: starts checkout, no order. Exits: places an order.

  1. After 1 hourC1 · Your basketA plain reminder with the item, the price and one button.
  2. After 24 hoursC2 · Make it a kitTurns the basket into the matching kit and shows the saving. Then the flow stops.

Launch campaign

Audience: engaged subscribers first, everyone else 48 hours later.

  1. Launch dayL1 · The first collectionEleven pieces in three colours, then the three kits.
03

Key decisions

Each choice protects something the store already does well.

A gift that is not a discount

The welcome offer is free delivery, not a percentage off. A code would stack on top of the 20% kit saving and weaken the idea that a kit is always the best deal.

Kits in every flow

W2 shows the kits, W3 the builder, C2 the kit that matches your basket. Every message nudges the same way as the store: more pieces, better price.

Two reminders, then silence

A new brand should not chase. The cart flow sends two emails in 24 hours and stops, and the second one adds a reason to come back rather than repeating the first.

No false urgency

No countdown timers and no “only 2 left”. Every claim in the emails is something the store really does: the 20%, the 15%, the £40.

One job per email

Each email has one button and one idea. They are short enough to read on a phone in the time it takes to queue for a coffee.

The brand rules still apply

Cream page, espresso buttons, square corners, spaced capitals. Terracotta appears only inside the logo, exactly as it does on the store and on the products.

05

Built to run in Klaviyo

Every email exists twice. The preview version uses sample data so it renders anywhere. The Klaviyo version, in emails/klaviyo/, swaps in merge tags and absolute image links, ready to paste into a custom HTML template.

Triggers

Welcome: added to the newsletter list. Cart: the “Checkout Started” metric from the store, with a flow filter that removes anyone who has placed an order since.

Merge tags

First name with a fallback, the checkout link, the unsubscribe link and the sender address. Not yet run against a live account, so the first test send comes before anything else.

Personalisation next

A “where do you train?” answer collected at sign-up would let W2 show only the matching kit through a conditional split.

One file per email

A short Python script, emails/build.py, generates all six from shared components, so a colour or footer change happens once.

Email-safe by default

Table layout, inline styles, a hidden preview text, alt text on every image, and a light-only colour scheme so the cream and espresso are never inverted.

Compliance

Every email carries an unsubscribe link and a sender address. The sign-up form needs clear consent wording before any real list is collected.

06

How I would measure it

Opens are unreliable now that Apple Mail pre-loads images, so each flow is judged on clicks and orders, with opens as a rough guide only.

FlowMain metricAlso watchFirst test
WelcomePlaced order rate within 14 daysClick rate on W1, unsubscribes after W3Free delivery against a percentage off in W1
Abandoned cartRecovered revenue per recipientShare of orders that became a kitC1 at 1 hour against 4 hours
LaunchRevenue per recipientClick rate by subject line, spam complaintsSubject A against subject B

The cart flow has one extra number worth checking: how many recovered orders were kits. If C2 only brings back the same single bag, the kit nudge is not working.

07

What's next

  • Build it in Klaviyo. Import the HTML, set up the three flows, and send test emails to check the merge tags and the way they look in Gmail, Apple Mail and Outlook.
  • Connect the store. Today the footer form is a front-end demo. It needs a real sign-up form, and the cart emails need a store that reports “Checkout Started”, which comes with the move to Shopify.
  • Add a post-purchase flow. A thank you, care and delivery information, and a gentle invitation to complete the set.

This is a concept project. Product images are mockups, prices are illustrative and no email has been sent to a real list.
