Case study · E-commerce

Designing an online store
with character.

embrava is a training gear brand built on four colours and one gesture: an m shaped like a wave. The challenge was to turn that identity into a store that feels like the brand, makes choosing colours effortless and nudges people towards bigger baskets.

01

The brief

The brand already existed: a logo, a palette, and a full product range made in the same four colours. What it lacked was a place to sell them.

  • Feel like embrava. The store should look like an extension of the brand board, not a generic template.
  • Make colour the hero. Every product comes in matching colours, so picking one should be instant and visual.
  • Raise the average order. Encourage people to buy sets rather than single pieces.
02

Respecting the brand

The wave-shaped m is the heart of the identity, so one rule guided the whole build: the mark is never redrawn. The original vector paths from the logo kit are embedded once as an SVG sprite and reused everywhere, only ever recoloured.

  • Dark#2A2624
  • Terracotta#C46848
  • Sand#D9C4A2
  • Cream#F4EEE3

The wave becomes the site's visual language: section markers, the hero backdrop, the gift card and even the empty basket. DM Sans pairs with the logo's geometric letterforms for all interface text.

03

Three directions, one choice

I explored three ways into the store before building anything.

A

Dark editorial

Big type, dark backgrounds, gym attitude. Strong, but it hid the warmth of the palette and made products harder to compare.

B

Warm catalogue

Cream background, clean cards, colour swatches on every product. The clearest way to shop.

Chosen as the base
C

Kit-led

A guided start: “Where do you train?” leads straight to bundles. Great for basket size.

Merged into the homepage

The final design combines B and C: a calm, colour-first catalogue, with kits leading the homepage to lift the average order.

04

Key decisions

Every feature exists to serve one of the three goals in the brief.

Colour-first shopping

Swatches swap the product photo in place. The chosen colour travels with you to the product page and into the link, so a shared product opens in the right colour.

Kits as the best deal

Three kits save 16–17%. A kit is always slightly cheaper than building the same set yourself, so the bundle stays the obvious choice.

Build your own kit

Pick any three pieces, in any colours, and unlock 15% off. A progress bar shows how close you are, turning the discount into a small goal.

A free delivery nudge

Free UK delivery starts at £50, just above a typical single order. The basket shows exactly how much is left, encouraging one more piece.

Pricing for a new brand

Launch prices sit in the mid-market: below the big names, clearly above budget gear. Premium enough to signal quality, honest enough for a first purchase.

The brand board, live

A full-width band of terracotta, dark and sand blocks carries the tagline across the page, echoing the original brand presentation.

06

Product pages that sell the set

Each product page answers the questions in the order people ask them: what is it, how much, which colour, then why buy more.

  • Gallery with a thumbnail per colour, linked to the swatches.
  • A kit offer appears automatically when the product belongs to one.
  • “Complete the look” suggests pieces from the same kit first.
07

Designed for the phone

A brand like this is mostly discovered on social media, on a phone. On mobile the layout stacks into a single column, buttons grow to thumb size, and a sticky “Add” bar follows you down the product page once the main button scrolls away.

08

Under the hood

No framework

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Fast to load, easy to read, nothing to install.

Data apart from design

Products, prices, kits and copy live in one file, structured so the catalogue can move to Shopify later.

One SVG sprite

The original logo paths are inlined once and reused, keeping the mark pixel-perfect at every size.

Accessible by default

Swatches behave as radio buttons for screen readers, focus is always visible, and motion respects reduced-motion settings.

Persistent basket

The basket survives page changes and reloads, with quantity controls and a free delivery tracker.

Responsive

Layouts adapt from wide desktop down to small phones, with no horizontal scrolling.

09
