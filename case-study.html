Case study · E-commerce

Designing an online store
with character.

Embrava is a training gear brand built on a warm palette, a sun-and-sea symbol and a clean, spaced wordmark. The challenge was to turn that identity into a store that feels like the brand, makes choosing colours effortless and nudges people towards bigger baskets.

01

The brief

The brand already existed: a logo, a palette, and a full product range made in the same four colours. What it lacked was a place to sell them.

  • Feel like Embrava. The store should look like an extension of the brand board, not a generic template.
  • Make colour the hero. Every product comes in matching colours, so picking one should be instant and visual.
  • Raise the average order. Encourage people to buy sets rather than single pieces.
02

From a wave-shaped m to sun and sea

The first identity drew the brand's m as a wave inside the word itself. It had personality, but it asked a lot of one letter: at small sizes, stitched or printed, it was hard to read and could look like a typo.

So I kept the name and the warm palette, gave the sea its own blue, and split the logo into two pieces that can live apart.

  • A clean wordmark. Spaced capitals in Jost Light, with no tricks, so it reads at any size.
  • A symbol of its own. A circle cut by a wave: a large sun setting over the sea. It no longer has to look like an m, and it works alone on caps, socks, labels and social media, with a weight designed for screen printing and embroidery.
  • Full colour, or one ink. On light pieces and backgrounds the logo is a terracotta sun over a blue sea; on dark and terracotta ones it turns to a single cream ink, so it always stands out and stays simple to stitch or print.
  • Warmth as the difference. Sand, terracotta and sea against the cold black of most fitness brands.

On product, the full lockup goes on bags, bottles and totes, and the symbol on its own where space is tight. Online, the symbol marks each section, the gift card and the empty basket, and Jost, the wordmark's typeface, sets all interface text.

03

Three directions, one choice

I explored three ways into the store before building anything.

A

Dark editorial

Big type, dark backgrounds, gym attitude. Strong, but it hid the warmth of the palette and made products harder to compare.

B

Warm catalogue

Cream background, clean cards, colour swatches on every product. The clearest way to shop.

Chosen as the base
C

Kit-led

A guided start: “Where do you train?” leads straight to bundles. Great for basket size.

Merged into the homepage

The final design combines B and C: a calm, colour-first catalogue, with kits leading the homepage to lift the average order. I then refined it into a quieter, light editorial finish: spaced capitals for every title, square corners, light catalogue cards and a neutral interface that leaves the colour to the products.

04

Key decisions

Every feature exists to serve one of the three goals in the brief.

Colour-first shopping

Swatches swap the product photo in place. The chosen colour travels with you to the product page and into the link, so a shared product opens in the right colour.

Kits as the best deal

Every kit saves 20%, against 15% for building your own. A kit is always the cheaper way to get the same pieces, so the bundle stays the obvious choice.

Build your own kit

Pick any three pieces, in any colours, and unlock 15% off. A progress bar shows how close you are, turning the discount into a small goal.

A free delivery nudge

Free UK delivery starts at £40, just above a typical single order. The basket shows exactly how much is left, encouraging one more piece.

Pricing for a new brand

Launch prices sit in the mid-market: below the big names, clearly above budget gear. Premium enough to signal quality, honest enough for a first purchase.

An editorial finish

Spaced capitals, square corners and quiet neutrals give the store a calm, fashion-led feel. Terracotta is kept for the symbol and the products, so they stand out.

06

Product pages that sell the set

Each product page answers the questions in the order people ask them: what is it, how much, which colour, then why buy more.

  • Gallery with a thumbnail per colour, linked to the swatches.
  • A kit offer appears automatically when the product belongs to one.
  • “Complete the look” suggests pieces from the same kit first.
07

Designed for the phone

A brand like this is mostly discovered on social media, on a phone. On mobile the layout stacks into a single column, buttons grow to thumb size, and a sticky “Add” bar follows you down the product page once the main button scrolls away.

08

Under the hood

No framework

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Fast to load, easy to read, nothing to install.

Data apart from design

Products, prices, kits and copy live in one file, structured so the catalogue can move to Shopify later.

One SVG sprite

The symbol and wordmark are inlined once and recoloured with CSS, so the logo stays sharp at every size and in every colour.

Accessible by default

Swatches behave as radio buttons for screen readers, focus is always visible, and motion respects reduced-motion settings.

Persistent basket

The basket survives page changes and reloads, with quantity controls and a free delivery tracker.

Responsive

Layouts adapt from wide desktop down to small phones, with no horizontal scrolling.
